# Working in this repository

This repository mechanises Wagner, Gierczak, Marshall, Li & Ahmed, *From Linearity
to Borrowing* (PACMPL 9:OOPSLA2:415, 2025), laid out as the paper is. `[CONF]` is
the conference paper; `[TR]` is its supplement, which prints the complete
definitions and proofs.

The task is to transcribe what is printed. It is not to find a proof of the same
theorem.

## Layout

- `Paper/` — the paper, section by section, in `[TR]`'s order: `S1_Syntax` …
  `S5_Model` (`Definitions.lean`, one row per printed definition),
  `S6_1_StandardLemmas` … `S6_8_FundamentalProperty` (`Lemmas.lean`, one record per
  numbered result; §6.2 also has `Definitions.lean` for Definitions 6.1–6.3),
  `Remarks.lean` files for the theorem-valued rows of §§4–5 whose proofs use §6,
  and `CONF/` for `[CONF]`'s own numbered results.
- `Paper/INDEX.md` — every numbered result and every definition row, with its
  status or tag, file and declarations.
- `Paper/LiteralReadings/` — measurements of printed items read literally, where
  the library uses a repaired reading. Nothing else depends on them.
- `Support/` — what the paper never prints, by topic (`Syntax/`, `Lifetimes/`,
  `Statics/`, `Dynamics/`, `Model/`, `LogicalRelation/`, `TypedWorld/`). Every file
  there is `[about ours]`.
- `docs/adjudications.md` — every departure from the printed text, argued: the
  printed form, the prose that grounds the reading, the reading adopted, and what
  the literal reading admits. Comments cite it by section (`§12.42`) or label
  (`G4`, `D6`).
- `comparator/` — the statement of `Fig16.LogRel.Typed.adequacy` over a verbatim
  copy of its trust base, for leanprover/comparator. `comparator/Challenge/**` is
  generated by `tools/challenge/run.sh`; never edit it by hand.
- `scripts/` — `check-hygiene.sh`, `CheckAxioms.lean`, `AxiomCheck.lean` and
  `axioms.expected`.

## Comments

A comment says what the thing is: the declaration, the printed text it
transcribes (quoted, with page), and its tag — `[as printed]`, `[encoding]`,
`[repair]`, `[variant: …]`, `[restricted: …]`, `[about ours: …]`. It does not
narrate how the declaration came to be or what was tried and not done. Where a
departure from the paper matters to a reader — a repaired definition, an added
hypothesis, a different proof route — the tag carries it and
`docs/adjudications.md` explains it. No counts, no changelog, no puffery.

## 1. Follow the printed proof. Never take a shorter route.

A printed proof is not stand-alone. It is where a technique gets built, and later
proofs reach for that technique. Proving a lemma some easier way leaves the
technique unbuilt, and the next lemma that needs it has nothing to reach for.

If a shorter route exists, that is not a reason to take it. Follow the page, step
by step, and build what each step names — even when the lemma would close without
it. If a shorter route is taken, record it in `docs/adjudications.md`, including
which printed technique was therefore not built. `[as printed]` scores the
statement; it says nothing about the proof.

## 2. The paper is not wrong.

Never report that a printed lemma is false, unsound, underivable, or has a gap —
not in prose, a declaration name, a comment, or a commit message. Say "cannot
reconcile", name the exact printed step, and look for what the mechanisation is
missing. A measurement of a literal reading describes what that reading admits.

## 3. Before inventing a route, check the objects are transcribed.

When a printed step will not follow, the first question is whether everything
that step names is transcribed. Reconstruction is invention.

`[TR]` numbers Definitions and Lemmas in two sequences — "6.2" is both Lemma 6.2
and Definition 6.2. A row present for a number is not evidence the object is
present; check both halves of `Paper/INDEX.md`.

## 4. Add hypotheses; never edit printed statements or definitions.

If a printed proof uses a fact its statement does not carry, add that fact as a
named hypothesis, quote the sentence it comes from, and leave the conclusion
exactly as printed. Prove any added hypothesis non-vacuous and pin its scope.
Never a hypothesis that assumes the lemma.

## 5. A blocker is a claim, and gets checked like one.

An obstruction is reported only as a named `def … : Prop` that elaborates, or a
configuration built in Lean with every hypothesis discharged. Otherwise: "not
derived, and no obstruction verified".

## 6. Do not split cases the paper does not split.

A printed case split is part of the proof. A split the paper does not make opens
obligations the paper never incurs. If the printed step is one sentence and the
mechanisation is a case tree, the case tree is probably not the paper's.

## 7. Hygiene

- No `axiom`, `sorry`, `native_decide`, `implemented_by`, `opaque`, `partial`,
  `unsafe` in `Paper/` or `Support/`. The one `sorry` in the repository is the body
  of the challenge statement in `comparator/Challenge.lean` (comparator's
  convention).
- Every declaration's `#print axioms` ⊆ `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.
- Every change ends with: `lake build` clean; `scripts/check-hygiene.sh` passing;
  `lake env lean scripts/AxiomCheck.lean` equal to `scripts/axioms.expected`; and,
  if a declaration of the adequacy statement's trust base changed,
  `tools/challenge/run.sh` rerun and `lake build Challenge` clean.
- `TR.lemma_6_N`, `TR.«name»` and `CONF.*` are aliases; the declaration each
  aliases is named in its record and in `Paper/INDEX.md`.
